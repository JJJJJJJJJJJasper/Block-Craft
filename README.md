# BlockCraft

A multiplayer voxel sandbox game that runs in the browser. It's built for a school Chromebook,
so it needs no installs and has a light GPU budget. You can share one link and your friends join the same world.
All textures and sounds are generated by code at startup; there are no image or audio files.

> **Renaming the game:** change `GAME_NAME` in `shared/config.js`. The menu, the page title and the server log all use it.

---

## Features

- **Infinite procedural worlds** made of 16×16×256 chunks, from a seed. There are 32 biomes modelled on the
  classic 1.12 overworld: Plains, Sunflower Plains, Forest, Flower Forest, Birch Forest, Roofed Forest, Taiga,
  Mega Taiga, Cold Taiga, Ice Plains, Ice Plains Spikes, Extreme Hills (+), Desert, Savanna (+ Plateau), Jungle,
  Jungle Edge, Swampland, Mesa (+ Plateau), Mushroom Island, Ocean, Deep Ocean, Frozen Ocean, River, Frozen River,
  the beaches, and the new Stony Peaks and Snowy Peaks. Biomes are big (often several hundred blocks across).
  Grass and leaves are tinted per biome.
- **Far terrain:** beyond your render distance a low-detail version of the land (snowy peaks, rock, forests, water)
  stretches out to about 900 blocks, so mountain ranges stand on the horizon. Distance haze and valley mist
  replace the old fog wall.
- **Mountain ranges:** long ranges with sharp ridges climb as high as ~240. Steep faces are bare rock, gentle slopes
  keep their grass, and everything above the snow line is snow (lower in cold places).
- **Underground:** winding cave tunnels, rooms, ravines, lava lakes at the bottom, and ores at realistic depths
  (coal, iron, gold, redstone, lapis, diamond, plus emerald in mountains). Granite, diorite and andesite pockets too.
- **Villages** in plains, desert, savanna, taiga and snowy plains: houses, big houses, a smithy, a church tower,
  wheat farms, lamp posts, roads and a well, with villagers.
- **Dungeons** underground: mossy rooms with a monster cage and one or two chests of loot.
  Also sugar cane by the water, pumpkins, vines and cocoa pods on jungle trees.
- **Structures** (our own designs in the classic spirit), each with its own chest loot:
  - **Mineshafts:** tunnels branching underground on fence-and-plank frames, with old rails, chests, and cobwebbed
    cave spider cages.
  - **Desert temples:** sandstone halls with striped towers. A shaft under the blue tile drops into a hidden room
    with four chests, and a pressure plate wired to TNT.
  - **Jungle temples:** mossy cobblestone on three floors, with vines, a lever puzzle and a trapped corridor.
  - **Witch huts:** cabins on stilts in swamps, each with a witch.
  - **Igloos:** snow domes with a bed and a furnace. Half have a trapdoor down to a basement with a chest and two
    prisoners.
  - **Ocean monuments:** prismarine temples on the deep sea floor, with wing towers, a gold core, a sponge room,
    guardians all around and three elder guardians inside.
  - **Woodland mansions:** huge three-floor dark oak houses in dark forests, full of rooms (bedrooms, libraries,
    storage, dining and wool rooms) and home to vindicators and evokers.
- **Landscape details:** small lakes sunk into the ground (water, rarely lava), cliffs that lean out over hollows in
  hilly country, mossy boulders in giant taigas, desert wells, buried fossils, and silverfish hiding in mountain
  stone.
- **Trees:** oak, big oak, birch, spruce, pine, mega spruce, dark oak, acacia, jungle (tall, bush and giant),
  swamp oak, plus huge mushrooms and ice spikes.
- **Fast rendering:** Web Workers generate, light and mesh chunks. Hidden faces are culled and identical
  neighbouring faces are merged (greedy meshing), with ambient occlusion. All textures share one atlas
  (a WebGL2 texture array), there's frustum culling, fog, and a render distance from 2 to 12 chunks.
- **Sky:** a shader-painted sky dome with a blue gradient by day, a glowing sun, orange and pink sunrises and sunsets,
  a crescent moon, twinkling stars and a faint galaxy band at night, drifting clouds, and green and purple
  **auroras** at night in snowy biomes.
- **Lighting and time:** a day/night cycle. Skylight and torch light are computed per block, so caves are dark and
  torches glow warm.
- **Blocks:** about 350 blocks. They include six wood types with logs that lie along the direction you place them,
  stairs and slabs (which stack into full blocks), doors, trapdoors, fences and fence gates, walls, glass panes,
  iron bars, beds, chests, ladders, vines, buttons, levers, pressure plates and rails. Also stained glass, carpets,
  concrete powder, glazed terracotta and beds in 16 colours, plus cake, flower pots, anvils, cauldrons, hoppers,
  TNT, pistons, note blocks, jukeboxes, prismarine, crops and saplings.
- **Items:** every item you can find or craft in the 1.12 overworld: tools and hoes in five materials, armor (including
  chainmail), bows and arrows, shields, buckets, shears, flint and steel, fishing rods, compasses, clocks, maps,
  books, minecarts, boats, music discs (with made-up names), paintings, item frames, plus all the foods and materials.
  Over 370 recipes.
- **Armor:** leather, gold, iron and diamond helmets, chestplates, leggings and boots. They reduce mob damage
  and wear out, with an armor bar above the hearts.
- **Interaction:** a block outline, hold-to-break with crack stages, placing, a 9-slot hotbar, an inventory (E),
  item drops you can pick up, and a classic-style inventory (armor slots, character preview, off-hand slot,
  2×2 crafting). There's a 3×3 crafting table, furnace smelting, and a creative inventory with Building,
  Decoration, Nature, Redstone, Transportation, Misc, Food, Tools, Combat, Brewing and Materials tabs plus search.
- **Using things:** open and close doors, trapdoors and gates. Chests store items, and dungeon chests fill with loot
  the first time you open them. Beds set your respawn point and skip the night once everyone sleeps. Hoes make
  farmland, seeds grow into crops, saplings grow into trees, and bone meal speeds both up. Buckets carry water and
  lava, and concrete powder hardens in water. Flint and steel lights TNT. Shears cut leaves and sheep, and a bucket
  on a cow gives milk. Bows shoot arrows (hold to draw), and snowballs, eggs and ender pearls can be thrown.
- **Survival:** health, hunger, fall damage, drowning, death (you drop your items) and respawning.
  **Creative:** infinite blocks, flying and instant breaking.
- **Mobs (36 kinds):**
  - **Animals:** pigs, cows, mooshrooms, sheep (in natural wool colours), chickens (they lay eggs), horses,
    donkeys, mules, llamas, wolves, jungle cats, rabbits, polar bears, parrots, squid and bats.
  - **Monsters:** zombies, husks, zombie villagers, skeletons, strays, spiders, cave spiders, slimes (they split
    when killed), witches (thrown potions), silverfish, guardians and elder guardians (beams), vindicators,
    evokers (fangs and vexes) and vexes.
  - **BlockCraft's own monsters:**
    - The **Rumbler** is an exploding stone beast. It creeps close, hisses and blows up, and it's afraid of cats.
    - The **Shade** is a tall, silent night wanderer in a dark cloak with one glowing slit. Look it in the eye or
      hit it, and it hunts you, blinking from place to place.
  - **Golems:** iron golems guard villages. Build one yourself from four iron blocks with a pumpkin on top. A snow
    golem is two snow blocks and a pumpkin, and it throws snowballs at monsters.
- **Mob AI:** mobs find their way around walls, holes and water (A* pathfinding) instead of walking into them.
  - Animals follow you while you hold their favourite food, and panic and run when hurt.
  - Wolf packs fight back together, and polar bears defend their cubs.
  - Zombies and illagers hunt villagers, and villagers run from them.
  - Skeletons keep their distance and shoot, and spiders are calm by day.
  - Monsters come out at night, and in dark caves at any time. Spawner cages keep producing their monster.
- **Breeding and taming:**
  - **Breeding:** feed two animals of the same kind their favourite food and they make a baby, which grows up in a
    few minutes. A horse and a donkey make a mule.
  - **Taming:** wolves take bones, cats take raw fish and parrots take seeds. Tamed wolves and cats follow you, sit
    when you right-click them, and wolves defend you.
  - **Horses, donkeys, mules and llamas:** climb on with an empty hand until one trusts you (treats help). Then
    saddle a horse, donkey or mule and ride it: it goes where you look, and Space jumps.
- **Leads, name tags and fireworks:**
  - **Leads:** right-click an animal to lead it on a rope. Right-click a fence to tie it up, and right-click again
    to take it back.
  - **Name tags:** right-click a mob and type a name. Named mobs show their name and never despawn.
  - **Firework rockets:** right-click a block to launch one. Each bursts in its own colours and shape (ball, big
    ball, star, ring or heart).
- **Music:** original, generated background music (calm piano and pads that change with the time of day and
  underground) plays now and then. Twelve music discs, each its own generated piece, play from jukeboxes and get
  louder as you get closer. Skeletons that shoot a Rumbler make it drop a disc. Set the music volume in Settings.
- **Minecarts and boats:**
  - **Minecarts:** rails join up and curve on their own, and climb slopes. Powered rails next to a redstone block,
    redstone torch or lever (it passes along up to 8 rails) speed you up, and unpowered ones brake.
  - **Boats** paddle across water.
  - Press Shift to get out of either.
- **Villagers and trading:** villagers have jobs (farmer, librarian, smith, butcher, cleric, fletcher), shown by
  their robes. Right-click one to trade with emeralds. Villages have houses with doors, beds and glass windows,
  big houses, libraries, a blacksmith's forge, a church tower, crop farms, animal pens, lamp posts and a well.
- **PvP:** in survival, players can fight each other.
- **Fancy graphics** (on by default, toggle in Settings): light rays from the sun, a golden-hour glow, colour
  grading and a vignette. Water reflects the sky and glints in the sun, and leaves and plants sway in the wind.
- **Held items:** the item you hold appears in your hand (it bobs as you walk and swings when you mine or place),
  and other players see it in their avatar's hand. Torches stick to the side of the block you click.
- **Procedural audio:** break, place, footsteps, hurt, pickup, eating and mob sounds. Press M to mute.
- **Multiplayer:** the server is authoritative. It handles rooms with 4-letter codes, invite links
  (`/?world=ABCD`), interpolated avatars with name tags, chat (T), a player list (Tab), host controls
  (game mode, time, kick) and automatic reconnects.
- **Persistence:** each world's edits, players, inventories and furnaces are saved to `data/<CODE>.json`.
  Saves happen every 30 s and on shutdown.
- **Anti-cheat checks:** limits on reach and movement speed, and rate limits on edits and chat.

## 1. Run it on your own computer

You need **Node.js 18 or newer** (https://nodejs.org, the "LTS" version).

```bash
npm install        # download the libraries (three.js, ws, vite) - once
npm run dev        # start the game in development mode (auto-reloads when you edit code)
```

Open **http://localhost:3000** in Chrome, type a name and click **Create World**.

For production mode (faster loading, which is what hosting sites run):

```bash
npm run build      # bundle the client into dist/
npm start          # serve dist/ + run the game server
```

Use the `PORT` environment variable to pick a different port, e.g. `PORT=8080 npm start`.

### Let friends on the same Wi-Fi join

1. Start the server on your computer (`npm run dev` or `npm start`). It prints something like:
   ```
   Local:   http://localhost:3000/
   Network: http://192.168.1.23:3000/
   ```
2. Create a world and press **Esc**. The pause menu shows the **world code**.
3. Your friends open `http://192.168.1.23:3000/?world=ABCD` on their Chromebooks, using *your* Network
   address and code.

If it doesn't connect:
- **Windows:** allow Node.js through the firewall when asked (or in *Windows Defender Firewall → Allow an app*).
- **macOS:** click *Allow* when asked about incoming connections.
- **School/public Wi-Fi** often blocks devices from talking to each other ("client isolation"). In that case,
  deploy online (next section) or use a phone hotspot.

## 2. Put it online for free (Render)

Render's free web services support WebSockets, which this game needs.

**Step 1 - Put the code on GitHub**
1. Create a free account at https://github.com and click **New repository** (e.g. `blockcraft`). Leave it empty.
2. In this project folder run:
   ```bash
   git init            # skip if it's already a git repo
   git add -A
   git commit -m "BlockCraft"
   git branch -M main
   git remote add origin https://github.com/YOUR-NAME/blockcraft.git
   git push -u origin main
   ```

**Step 2 - Deploy on Render**
1. Create a free account at https://render.com (sign in with GitHub).
2. Click **New + → Blueprint**, pick your `blockcraft` repository and click **Apply**.
   Render reads `render.yaml` from this project and sets everything up.
   *Or create it by hand:* **New + → Web Service** → pick the repo, then set
   - Runtime: **Node**
   - Build Command: `npm install && npm run build`
   - Start Command: `npm start`
   - Instance Type: **Free**
3. Wait for the build (2-4 minutes). Render shows your URL, like `https://blockcraft-abcd.onrender.com`.
4. Open it, create a world, press **Esc → Copy invite link**, and send the link to your friends.

**Important things to know about the free plan**
- The server **goes to sleep after ~15 minutes with nobody connected**. The next visitor waits about a minute while it wakes up.
- Free instances have **no permanent disk**. Saved worlds are lost whenever the server restarts, sleeps or
  redeploys. Worlds survive while people are playing, and the menu offers to recreate a missing world code
  with a fresh map. To keep worlds permanently, switch the service to a paid instance and attach a
  **Disk** (instructions are in the comments of `render.yaml`), then set `DATA_DIR` to the disk's mount path.

### Alternative: Railway

1. Push to GitHub as above, then at https://railway.com click **New Project → Deploy from GitHub repo**.
2. Railway detects Node and runs `npm install`, `npm run build` and `npm start` automatically.
3. Under **Settings → Networking**, click **Generate Domain** to get a public URL.
4. To keep worlds: right-click the service → **Attach Volume**, mount it at `/data`, and add the variable
   `DATA_DIR=/data`.
   (Railway gives trial credit; after that it's a low-cost paid plan. Check their pricing page.)

## Controls

| Key | Action | Key | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| WASD | Move | Mouse | Look |
| Space | Jump / swim up | Space twice | Toggle flying (creative) |
| Shift | Sneak: slow, can't fall off edges (descend when flying) | W twice | Sprint (keep holding W) |
| Left click (hold) / **R** | Break block / attack | Right click / **F** | Place / use doors, chests, beds, tables / eat / wear armor / throw |
| Right click (hold, with a bow) | Draw the bow, release to shoot | Shift + right click | Place a block against a chest/door instead of using it |
| 1-9, mouse wheel | Choose hotbar slot | E | Inventory and crafting |
| Q | Drop one item | T or / | Chat (host commands start with /) |
| Tab | Player list | F3 or ` | FPS / debug info |
| M | Mute | Esc | Pause menu (settings, invite link, host controls) |

**Chromebook tips**
- The top-row keys are browser keys (F3 = refresh!). Use **`** for the debug screen, or hold **Search** for F-keys.
- Trackpad: **tap** = break/attack, **two-finger tap** or **Alt + tap** = place. You can also use **R** to break and **F** to place.
- Ctrl is deliberately unused, because Ctrl+W closes the tab.
- If it's slow, lower the render distance in **Esc → Settings** (4 is a good value for older Chromebooks).
  Leave "Sharp rendering on high-DPI screens" off.

**Host commands** (the first player in a world is the host; also available in Esc → Host):
`/time day|noon|sunset|night`, `/mode survival|creative`, `/tp <x> <y> <z>`, `/kick <name>`,
`/summon <mob>` (e.g. `/summon horse`), `/locate <structure>` (village, mineshaft, desert_temple, jungle_temple,
witch_hut, igloo, monument, mansion: tells you the nearest one's coordinates).

**Riding, taming and more**
- Right-click an animal while holding its food to breed or tame it, or holding a lead, saddle or name tag to use it.
- Right-click a horse with an empty hand to climb on. Once it's tamed and saddled: W to ride, look to steer, Space to
  jump, Shift to get off.
- Right-click a tamed wolf or cat to make it sit or stand.

## How the code is organised

```

  items.js      tools, food, materials, mining-speed formula
  recipes.js    crafting + smelting recipes

  vehicles.js     minecarts and boats
  projectiles.js  arrows, snowballs, eggs, ender pearls, witch potions

  persistence.js  save/load JSON files in data/

  index.html, style.css, main.js
  engine/     game.js (main loop), player.js (movement), input.js
  world/      world.js (chunk streaming), worker.js, workerPool.js, mesher.js, lighting.js
  rendering/  renderer.js (Three.js + chunk shader), textures.js (procedural art), sky.js, entities.js,

  ui/         menu.js, hud.js, inventory.js, chat.js, settings.js

  audio/      sfx.js (Web Audio sound synthesis), music.js (generated music)

```

### The big ideas (worth reading in this order)

1. **`shared/terrain.js`:** noise functions turn into landscapes. Each column's height mixes a slow
   "continent" noise with detail noise, scaled by a "hilliness" noise. Caves are where two 3D noise fields
   are both near zero. Because the generator is a *pure function* of the seed, the browser and the server
   produce identical chunks. The network only ever sends **edits** (which blocks players changed), never terrain.
2. **`client/world/mesher.js`:** face culling and greedy meshing reduce ~200,000 possible faces per chunk to a
   few hundred rectangles.
3. **`client/world/lighting.js`:** light spreads by breadth-first search, like a flood fill.
4. **`client/rendering/renderer.js`:** the shader computes `max(skylight × daylight, torchlight)`, so the
   day/night cycle costs nothing.
5. **`server/room.js`:** the server decides what really happened. Clients *ask* to break or place, and the
   server checks the request and tells everyone.
6. **`client/rendering/entities.js`:** other players are drawn 100 ms in the past and interpolated between
   network snapshots, so they move smoothly even though updates only arrive 20 times a second.

### Adding a new block

1. Add it at the **end** of the family list in `shared/blocks.js` (new blocks get ids from 1024 upward in the order
   they're written, so never insert in the middle or saved worlds will mix up):
   ```js
   cube('mossy_stone', { tex: { all: 'mossy_stone' }, hardness: 1.5, tool: 'pickaxe', harvest: 1, sound: 'stone', cat: 'building' });
   ```
   Blocks with several states (a door's open/closed, a stair's direction) are a `family`: each combination is its
   own id. Their shapes are lists of boxes in `shared/shapes.js`, used for both drawing and collisions.
2. Add a painter with the same texture name in `PAINTERS` in `client/rendering/textures.js`:
   ```js
   mossy_stone(img, r) { PAINTERS.stone(img, r); speckle(img, hex('#4a7a2a'), 0.25, r); },
   ```
   (Or tint the wool pattern with `tex: { all: 'wool:#hexcolor' }` and skip this step.)
3. Optionally add a recipe in `shared/recipes.js`. The block shows up in the creative inventory automatically.

## Testing

```bash
npm test
```

This starts the real server with a temporary data folder and connects two headless clients. It then checks
that placed and broken blocks reach the other player, late joiners receive edits, out-of-reach edits are
rejected, chat and movement sync, and an edit survives a server restart.

## Known limitations

- **Inventories are client-side.** The server saves your inventory but doesn't verify it, so a determined player
  could edit their own items. Reach, speed and edit rate *are* enforced, and furnace contents and dropped items
  are server-owned.
- Water and lava don't flow, and sand and gravel don't fall. A water bucket never runs dry (the ocean is endless).
- **Redstone is decorative:** dust, torches, repeaters, buttons, levers, pressure plates, pistons, hoppers,
  dispensers and droppers can be placed but don't power anything. Iron doors open by hand for that reason.
- Maps, item frames, paintings, armor stands and the brewing items exist as items (craftable or found in loot)
  but can't be used yet. Chest, furnace, hopper and TNT minecarts ride like plain minecarts.
- Furnaces and pumpkins show their front on two sides.
- Other players don't show armor on their avatars yet (your own preview in the inventory does).
- The world generator is our own design, so a seed gives a different world than it would in other block games.
- Free hosting forgets worlds when the server restarts (see the Render notes above).
- Day and night lighting is updated by the shader, but block light is recomputed only when a chunk is remeshed.
  That's instant for normal edits, but with many simultaneous edits there can be a short delay.
